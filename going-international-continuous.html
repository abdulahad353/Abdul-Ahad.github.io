Project 03 · International markets · European Commission project

Turning SME research into internationalisation decisions.

Going International was a project designed to identify, select and support Argentinean SMEs with high growth potential. My contribution focused on structured research, SME readiness, strategic diagnosis, competitor benchmarking and international positioning.

The challenge

International growth requires more than market demand.

The project documentation sets out a programme to identify and select 40 Argentinean small and medium-sized enterprises across Buenos Aires, Córdoba, Mendoza and Rosario, then support their international competitiveness. The central question was not simply whether an overseas market looked attractive, but whether each company had the strategic direction, organisation and operating capabilities to pursue growth.

The project proposed a staged funnel to assess applicants consistently, moving from basic eligibility and strategic screening to readiness, execution capability and management commitment. The wider programme also outlined international development planning over a three-year horizon.

My contribution

Turn company and market information into a structured diagnosis.

My contribution centred on research and analysis within the team project. I worked with the project methodology to examine SME information, business models and value propositions, and to connect external market opportunities with internal capabilities and internationalisation readiness.

The work included market and competitor research, strategic diagnosis, benchmarking and the development of recommendations. The project framework also considered organisational structure and governance, financial and operational capabilities, strategic orientation, and the practical requirements of international expansion.

Methods and frameworks

Use complementary lenses to assess readiness.

PESTEL was used to structure the external environment; SWOT to organise strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats; VRIO to consider resources and capabilities; and Porter’s Five Forces to examine competitive structure. These frameworks supported a broader assessment that included market attractiveness, international competitive benchmarking, differentiation, positioning and entry feasibility.

The programme design also described questionnaires and scorecards for early screening, qualitative assessment of leadership motivation and commitment, and deeper consideration of execution factors such as financial resilience, operational scalability, organisational stability and supply-chain robustness.

From diagnosis to action

Connect strategic recommendations with execution.

The project’s proposed second phase moved beyond selection into strategic and organisational diagnosis, international benchmarking and market positioning, and the creation of a three-year international development roadmap. The roadmap framework considered market prioritisation, capability development, operational adjustments, phasing, governance and monitoring.

This helped reinforce an important point: a promising market is only one part of an internationalisation decision. A company also needs a credible value proposition, sufficient resources, management alignment and the ability to deliver consistently across borders.

What I learned

Expansion is a capability question as well as a market question.

The project strengthened my understanding of how market research, competitive analysis and internal business assessment can be brought together to support a more disciplined decision. It also highlighted the value of making assumptions explicit, comparing companies through a consistent method, and translating findings into practical priorities rather than relying on market opportunity alone.
