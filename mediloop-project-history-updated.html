Project 01 · Healthcare · Strategy + Finance

From a healthcare problem to a business system.

Mediloop was a team project exploring a connected way to help people manage medication. My contribution focused on understanding the market, comparing competing solutions and building the business case: how the product could work as a service, who it could serve, how it might earn revenue and what risks would need to be addressed.

The problem and concept

Medication management is a connected journey.

Taking medication as prescribed can be difficult to manage consistently, especially when several doses, reminders or refills are involved. Caregivers may also be unsure whether a dose was taken. The project started from a simple observation: a dispenser on its own would not address the full journey.

The concept brought together a smart dispenser, an app for reminders and tracking, alerts for caregivers when a dose was missed, and a link to pharmacy refills or delivery. The proposed journey ran from scheduling and filling the tray to dispensing, reminders, alerts and refills.

My contribution

Research the market. Compare alternatives. Build the business case.

I worked on the business plan and investment proposal, connecting the needs of patients, caregivers and pharmacies with the practical decisions behind the service. I mapped how scheduling, filling, dispensing, reminders, missed-dose alerts and refills could work together, and considered where the process would need to be reliable and easy to use.

Competitor analysis: I compared the proposed Mediloop model with pill dispensers, medication reminder apps and traditional pharmacy services. I looked at which parts of the medication journey each alternative covered, including smart dispensing, caregiver visibility, reminders and refill support. This comparison helped identify Mediloop’s intended point of difference: bringing the steps together instead of solving only one part of the problem.

Market and commercial analysis: I worked with the project’s market-sizing assumptions and assessed the proposed rental, purchase and subscription options. I reviewed the unit economics and five-year financial forecast to understand how pricing, product costs and operating expenses could affect the business model.

Risk and investment planning: I considered risks including supply-chain disruption, regulatory and compliance delays, market adoption, competition and operational execution. I also worked with the funding proposal, which outlined how a potential €600,000 could be allocated across manufacturing, team and operations, product development, go-to-market activity and working capital.

Commercial model

Explore purchase and rental options.

The financial model compared three options: renting the device for €29 per month, buying it for €249, or buying it for €249 with an optional €19 monthly subscription. These were proposed prices for the business plan, not prices from a live commercial launch.

The model estimated a landed cost of €155 per device. Against a proposed €249 selling price, that would leave €94 gross profit per unit, or a 37.8% gross margin before other operating costs. The presentation estimated a €28B+ total addressable market and a €3.6B+ serviceable market. These figures were part of the project’s assumptions, rather than independently validated market results.

Skills demonstrated

Turning research into practical business decisions.

This project gave me practical experience in competitor benchmarking, market research, business model development, pricing analysis, unit economics, financial forecasting, risk assessment and investment planning. It also required me to connect the findings: a competitive advantage only matters if customers value it, the service can be delivered, and the economics have a credible path to working.

I learned to treat assumptions critically rather than present projections as results. The market estimates, pricing, margins and funding requirement were part of a proposal, not validated commercial outcomes. Testing them with users, pharmacies and other potential partners would be an important next step.

Building the business case behind the idea.

Working on Mediloop gave me a chance to bring customer needs, service design, financial assumptions and risks into one business plan. The project work concluded on 30 June 2026.
