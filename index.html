Project 01 · Mediloop

From a healthcare problem to a business system.

This project was developed as a business plan and investment proposal. I worked across the operating and commercial logic needed to make the idea coherent — problem, ecosystem, business model, finance, funding, risk and execution.

The work

Map the medication journey, understand the ecosystem and connect product decisions to commercial and operational reality.

Project status
Investment proposal

A business plan developed to structure the opportunity and pitch the project for investment.

My contribution

Operating model · strategy · finance · commercial logic · execution planning

Core question

How do you make medication adherence easier for patients while creating visibility for caregivers and a connected refill journey?

What I did

Connected the pieces.

I mapped the patient, caregiver and pharmacy journey; translated the problem into the Schedule → Fill → Dispense → Remind → Alert → Refill flow; worked through the business model; developed market and financial logic; considered risks, partnerships and circularity; and helped shape the funding story.

What I learned

Good products need system thinking.

A strong product idea is not enough. Healthcare products have to work across users, caregivers, pharmacies, operations, economics, compliance and trust. I learned to test the business system around the product — not only the product itself.

Project 02 · Fantitos

Researching what makes social play feel worth repeating.

This is an ongoing product project. I am using research, interviews and experimentation to understand the user problem before deciding what the product should become.

What I did

Started with users, not features.

I conducted 20 semi-structured interviews with university students and young professionals in Spain and India; grouped recurring problems; translated those findings into product hypotheses; explored AI-powered personalisation and game modes; and worked through market size, MAU scenarios and monetisation options.

What I learned

Personalisation only matters if it solves a real frustration.

The research reinforced the importance of listening for repeated pain points — repetitive gameplay, awkward content, limited variety and setup friction — rather than assuming that adding AI automatically creates value.

Still being tested.

Product, positioning, monetisation and growth assumptions continue to evolve as the work progresses.

Project 03 · Going International

Turning SME information into internationalisation decisions.

A European Commission project focused on supporting Argentinean SMEs. My contribution centred on structured research, diagnosis, benchmarking and strategic analysis.

What I did

Analysed readiness and competitive position.

I worked with the project methodology to assess SME information, support screening and selection, examine business models and organisational readiness, apply VRIO and SWOT, review financial and operational capability, analyse market attractiveness and benchmark competitors using tools including Porter 5 Forces and PESTEL.

What I learned

International growth is a capability problem, not only a market problem.

The project showed me that entering a new market depends on internal readiness too: governance, resources, value proposition, differentiation, operations, management alignment and the ability to execute the roadmap.

Project perspective

Research → diagnosis → positioning → roadmap.

The work connected market context with company-level capability, so recommendations could be grounded in both the external opportunity and the SME's ability to deliver.
